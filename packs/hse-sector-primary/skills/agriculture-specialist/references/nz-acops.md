# NZ agriculture ACOPs — roles & responsibilities, and safe farm vehicle operation

Regulatory-anchor reference for `agriculture-specialist` (NZ). Summarises WorkSafe NZ's two
agriculture **approved codes of practice (ACOPs)**, both approved under **HSWA 2015 s 222** and both
in effect from **1 April 2027**:

- **"Roles and responsibilities in agriculture"** — approved 31 August 2026.
- **"Safe farm vehicle operation"** — approved 3 September 2026.

Both apply to livestock, dairy, arable, **horticulture, viticulture** and mixed farming, so
`horticulture-specialist` uses them too. Neither covers purely domestic or recreational activity that
is unrelated to farm work. Crown copyright. This file summarises them with attribution and is not a
copy. Last verified **2026-10-04**. Confirm the current edition before relying on it.

> **Not legal advice.** The ACOPs set out **one** way to comply. You can meet the same duty another
> way if the standard is at least equal to the ACOP's.

---

## 1. Legal status — the safe-harbour timing trap

Both ACOPs are written as if the **HSW Amendment Act 2026** changes to **s 226** ("safe harbour")
already apply. Under those changes, a PCBU that follows **all the actions relevant to its
circumstances** is treated as having met the stated duty (s 226(2)).

**But both were approved before 1 April 2027, so neither has safe-harbour status yet.** WorkSafe
says each must be **reapproved after 1 April 2027** to get it. Until then, following the ACOP is
**evidence** that you met the duty. It is not deemed compliance.

When you advise on these ACOPs, say which applies:
- before reapproval: strong evidence
- after reapproval: safe harbour

Then check WorkSafe for the reapproval status. For the general rule →
`worksafe-nz-specialist` (law hierarchy).

Both ACOPs tell the reader to work out which actions are relevant by asking these questions. Use
them as the opening screen:

1. What is your role?
2. What are the roles of the other PCBUs?
3. What is the work?
4. What are the work conditions?
5. What are the risks, and which are **critical risks**?
6. What is your degree of **influence and control**?

## 2. Roles and responsibilities in agriculture

**Duties covered:**
- **s 34** — consult, cooperate and coordinate with other PCBUs that have the same duty
- **s 45** — worker duties
- **GRWM Regs reg 18** — worker PPE duties
- **GRWM Regs Part 4** — young persons

The ACOP is organised by role: Part B farmers, Part C farm workers, Part D contractors, Part E
suppliers and installers. It includes 20 worked farm scenarios, for example a shared shearing shed,
a contracting chain, an orchard with several PCBUs, and a contractor's worker using the farmer's
tractor.

**Key points:**

- **Tasks can be delegated. Legal duties cannot.** Agreeing who does an action never transfers a
  PCBU's duty, and the ACOP says to state this when you agree the split.
- **"Farmer" is the PCBU that operates the farm.** This can be an owner-operator, a sharemilking or
  contract-milking business, or a business engaged to manage the farm for an owner.
- **A landowner who doesn't operate the farm is not "the farmer".**
- **A farm manager is usually a worker.** They can hold delegated day-to-day responsibility, but the
  PCBU duties stay with the farm's PCBU. If the position is unclear, get legal advice.
- **One person can hold several roles.** For example, a farmer who is also a worker, or a contractor
  who is both a PCBU and a worker.
- **Overlapping duties:** each PCBU manages the shared matter **as far as it can influence and
  control it**. You are likely to have influence and control if you can:
  - direct how and when the work is done
  - choose the plant
  - control access to the site
- **Cooperation scales with size (from 1 April 2027).** A **large PCBU (20+ workers)** cooperates on
  all relevant risks. A **small PCBU (<20)** must cooperate on **critical risks** and may choose to
  cooperate on others. A contract can require more, but it can't remove HSWA duties. For the
  mechanics → `worksafe-nz-specialist:references/overlapping-duties.md`.
- **Recreational access:** the duty to manage or control the workplace applies only if:
  - recreation is part of the business (for example, farm walks or treks), or
  - farm work is happening near the recreational activity.

  An event that another PCBU runs on your land can create overlapping duties.

**The cooperation cycle (farmer and contractor tables, condensed):**

| When | Actions |
|---|---|
| **Before work** | Identify the other PCBUs. Swap hazard information both ways: farm-specific hazards to the contractor, and the risks the contractor's work creates back to the farmer. Agree roles, risks, which risks are critical, duties, and any shared duty. Confirm who has influence and control. Agree how you will communicate. |
| **Agree** | Allocate each action to the party with influence and control. **Record it in a form that suits the job**, for example a text, email, job sheet, farm diary note, induction record, traffic plan or written agreement. Contractors also brief their own workers and subcontractors on access routes, work areas, exclusion zones, emergency procedures and stop-work rules. |
| **During** | Update each other when conditions change. Check that agreed controls are in place before the work starts. **Stop, pause or re-plan** if the controls stop working. |
| **After / long jobs** | Check that the controls work. Update the agreement when the work, people, plant, timing or conditions change. |

**Workers (s 45, reg 18):**
- Follow farm instructions.
- Stop and ask if you are unsure.
- Report hazards and faults.
- Warn others about new risks.
- Ask for information in a form you can understand.
- Wear the PPE provided, and report PPE that is damaged or doesn't fit.
- If you are allowed to use farm equipment in your own time, follow the farm's rules.

**Suppliers and installers:** the ACOP covers only how they cooperate with the farmer on site. It
does not cover their upstream duties under ss 39–43.

## 3. Safe farm vehicle operation

**Duties covered:**
- **s 36** — primary duty
- **s 38** — fixtures, fittings and plant
- **GRWM Regs reg 9** — information, supervision, training and instruction
- **reg 13** — first aid
- **reg 14** — emergency plan
- **reg 21** — remote or isolated work
- **reg 46** — young persons and machinery
- **reg 47** — young persons and vehicles

**Vehicles covered:**
- quad bikes
- side-by-sides
- two-wheel motorbikes
- utes and 2WD/4WD vehicles
- tractors
- attached machinery that affects stability, such as trailers, mowers and spray units

The ACOP says farm vehicle use is **likely to be a critical risk** under HSWA.

**Not covered:**
- mobile plant from other industries, such as excavators, bulldozers and cranes
- farm buildings, yards and livestock-handling areas

**Structure:**
- **Part B** — the operating environment: minimum checks before starting, monitoring during work,
  and **pause → reassess → change or stop**.
- **Part C** — operating vehicles:
  - choose the right vehicle for today's job
  - pre-operation checks
  - manufacturer limits
  - controls for each vehicle type
  - higher-risk tasks: towing, spraying and spreading, water and ditches, raised loads,
    breakdowns and rollovers
  - maintenance
- **Part D** — training, remote work, emergency plans, first aid, young workers, and drugs and
  alcohol.

**Actions that change or tighten earlier NZ practice:**

| Topic | ACOP action |
|---|---|
| **Seatbelts** | Require them to be worn **wherever fitted**, on vehicles and on machinery. |
| **Helmets** | **Provide approved helmets and require them to be worn** on quad bikes, **side-by-sides** (drivers *and* passengers) and two-wheel motorbikes. "Approved" means a motorbike helmet to **NZS 5430**, or for off-road farm use (generally <30 km/h) an ATV helmet to **NZS 8600:2002**, or an equivalent standard. Replace a helmet after an impact or damage, or if it no longer fits. |
| **Quad bikes** | Set up to manufacturer specs. Use tyres and pressures that suit the terrain and load. Keep loads and spray tanks within rated limits. **No passengers.** Carry no load or animal that affects stability, for example a lamb across the lap. Don't use on terrain where stability is reduced. Consider engineering controls such as speed limiters, GPS monitoring and roll detection. Keep children well clear. Remove keys to stop unauthorised use. |
| **CPDs** | **Still a WorkSafe recommendation, not a required ACOP action.** WorkSafe "strongly recommends" a compatible CPD, installed and maintained to its maker's instructions. |
| **Children on quads** | **No one under 16 on an adult-sized quad.** Under 16 → an age- and size-appropriate quad, trained and supervised, with helmet and footwear, no passengers or destabilising loads, and set limits on area, speed and terrain. |
| **Side-by-sides** | **No one under 16 may operate one.** Sound ROPS. Seatbelts used by everyone. Manufacturer nets or doors fitted. Passengers **only in fitted seats with seatbelts**. Nothing carried in the footwell. Park on slopes to prevent rollaway. |
| **Tractors** | ROPS designed for the tractor. Seatbelt worn whenever the tractor moves. PTO guards fitted and refitted, and shafts capped when not in use. Hitching points and implements matched to the tractor's capacity. No passengers unless there is a fitted passenger seat with ROPS and a seatbelt. Park brake on before leaving the seat. |
| **Modifications** | Get manufacturer approval for any change that affects stability, control or safety systems. If there is no manufacturer, get **CPEng certification**. |
| **Maintenance** | Isolate before work: key out and kept with you, stored hydraulic and water pressure released. Use rated stands, never makeshift supports. Take unsafe vehicles out of service **immediately**. |

**Young workers (GRWM regs 46–47, set as ACOP actions):**

| Age | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Under 15** | Must not drive **any** vehicle, ride on a vehicle that is towing or using an implement, or ride on implements (except sleds or trailers designed mainly for carrying people or goods). Must not work at or with machinery, or help someone else do it. **Exception: tractors used for agricultural work**, as below. |
| **Under 12** | Must not drive or ride on tractors. |
| **12–14** | May drive or ride on a tractor **only in a fitted driver or passenger position**, if trained or being trained, and only if the task, terrain and conditions suit the child's age and capability. |
| **Any young worker (lawful work)** | Match the task to the young worker's capability. Train them for the specific vehicle. Supervise until they are competent. Set limits on speed, terrain, load and tasks. Increase supervision as the risk rises. |

For these rules, "vehicle" means a tractor or any self-propelled mobile plant, **except** cars,
trucks, motorcycles and machinery of 700 kg or less. A young person doing non-work activity on a
farm, such as collecting eggs for the family, is not doing farm work. The Roles ACOP covers children
who are present on the farm but not working.

## 4. Critical questions to consider

- Which ACOP actions are **relevant to this farm and task**, and are **all** of them done? Safe
  harbour, once the ACOP is reapproved, needs all relevant actions.
- Is each PCBU on the job **small or large**? Which shared risks are **critical**?
- Is the farmer–contractor agreement **recorded**, and does it say the duties stay put?
- Are helmets **provided and required** on quads, side-by-sides and motorbikes? Do they meet NZS
  5430 or NZS 8600?
- Is anyone **under 16** on an adult quad or driving a side-by-side? Is anyone **under 15** driving
  any vehicle other than a tractor (12–14, fitted seat, trained)?
- Are passengers carried only in **fitted seats with seatbelts**?
- Have any vehicle modifications been approved by the manufacturer or certified by a CPEng?
